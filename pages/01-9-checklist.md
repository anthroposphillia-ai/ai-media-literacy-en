## The 8-principle judging checklist

### Three steps

1. **Copy the sentence verbatim** — no summarising.
2. **Tag the principles it breaks** — often three or four (e.g. `①③`).
3. **Write the corrected sentence** — if you cannot, you have not finished judging.

### Checklist

| Principle | What to ask | ✓ |
|---|---|---|
| **③ Technical limits** | Are task, comparison set, conditions and data range all present? Does an independent board agree? | ☐ |
| **⑤ Interests** | "A said it" or "A did it"? What does the speaker gain? Any unsourced statistics? | ☐ |
| **⑦ Sources** | Is there a link to the original, and does it agree? Official, anonymous or self-reported? | ☐ |
| **④ Context** | Denominator? Comparison? How far can it be extended? | ☐ |
| **⑧ Alternatives** | Who decided? Any deferral or refusal? Can the reader do anything? | ☐ |
| **⑥ Cost and benefit** | Is a cost-bearing party named? A new risk? Benefit stated only as a multiple? | ☐ |
| **① Anthropomorphism** | Is the subject AI or a person? Does the verb attribute intent? | ☐ |
| **② Responsibility** | Named by name/title? Is a multi-branch responsibility collapsed? | ☐ |

### Twelve red flags (check the headline first)

1. "AI did X" — verb of intent
2. "world first · record · superhuman" — no comparison conditions
3. "according to industry sources" — promoted anonymity
4. "a survey found" (no organisation or sample) — no source
5. A percentage with no denominator
6. "unstoppable · already under way · on the treadmill" — determinism
7. "AI-driven layoffs" — check whether the company ever said so
8. Sentences nearly identical to the press release
9. "could" and "did" used interchangeably
10. No link to the original
11. Caveats present in the foreign original but missing locally
12. Responsible party written as "the system" or "the algorithm"

### Verdict scale

| Principles broken | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | accurate — quotable as is |
| 2–3 | partly wrong — quote with the conditions |
| 4+ | hold — check the original yourself |

> One last rule: **the article you criticise is also partly right.** State that part too, or the judgement reads as an attack rather than a review. That is why every card in this book pairs "what is wrong" with "corrected".
