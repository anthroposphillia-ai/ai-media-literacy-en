## ④ Give context

**Definition** — A number means something only **within its comparison and its range**. Turn one study's result, or one event's day, into a trend and it stops being information.

**Measured frequency** — **18 cards (17%)**.

### Why

Numbers gain power when scaled. "One model ranked first on one benchmark" → "the legal AI landscape has changed". "3,000 corporate roles cut" → "AI is replacing office work". Scaling usually happens through two deletions: the comparison and the range.

| Scaling move | Sentence | What to restore |
|---|---|---|
| Comparison deleted | "19.17% pass rate, first place" | the independent board's rank |
| Range deleted | "3,000 jobs cut" | share of total, which unit is affected |
| Date deleted | "AI investment collapsed" | the two periods compared, who measured |
| One case → all | "AI deceived a person" | number of cases, reproducibility |

### Three check questions

1. What is the **denominator**?
2. What is the **comparison**, and is it fair?
3. How far can this result be **extended** (one model? one company? one country?)?

### Real case

> The same GLM-Legal story, read this time for ④.

**What is wrong** — "19.17%, first place" hides three things at once: the **denominator** (a task pass rate means an 80.83% failure rate), the **comparison** (7th of 79 systems on the independent board), and the **range** (nothing here shows contract-review or case-law performance, which is what practitioners would need).

**Corrected**

> Inside the vendor's table 19.17% is first; on the same benchmark's independent leaderboard (79 systems) the model is 7th. A 19.17% task pass rate **includes an 80.83% failure rate**, and neither figure establishes performance on contract review or case-law research.

### One sentence that blocks scaling

> `This result comes from [task/sample/period]; under [other conditions/boards] the result differs.`
