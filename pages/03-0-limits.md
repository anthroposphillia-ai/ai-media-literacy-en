## Limits, and where our own calls were wrong

### Three calls we got wrong

**1. A Swedish broadcast deletion — we moved an early report to fact.** On 2026-10-14, Swedish daily Aftonbladet, citing unnamed sources, reported that an AI agent given server access by producer Mastiff deleted an entire edited season. Our card kept the actor as "an AI agent". **Hours later** TV4's head of programmes denied it — "it was not an AI agent acting on its own; that is not true" — and the broadcaster's investigation later attributed the loss to a **human factor (a failed backup)** at the production company. We corrected the card while writing this book.

**2. Sixty-three of our own cards have no source link.** Our audit record came in two generations: 27 verified cards carry links; **80 practice cards record only outlet and headline**. We demand ⑦ of every article while our own teaching material fails it. The book states outlet, headline and date instead of pretending a link exists.

**3. One edition with nine cards and one link.** On 2026-10-15 we produced nine cards but secured a single original link (other editions had 5–11). The lesson: source-grade discipline that is not enforced **by a form** collapses on a busy day — and our record had **no field for corrections**, so a correction could only be recorded by hand, months later.

### Six limits of this book

1. **Concentration** — the 11 case studies cover nine days (2026-10-09 to 17); four of them account for 15 of 27 cards. This is a slice, not a sample.
2. **Probabilistic collection** — articles arrive via RSS search. What the query misses effectively does not exist; one outlet dominates because its feed is stable; YouTube, newsletters and forums are outside this book.
3. **The judge has interests** (below).
4. **Access limits** — paywalled papers, deleted posts and login-walled material went unchecked, and we did not count how many cases we dropped for that reason.
5. **Cases age** — models, prices and rankings are October 2026 values. Quote them with dates. The retirement rule: 3-year-old cards are replaced at ~30% per year, while the principles stay.
6. **Not a verdict** — this is sentence-level judgement, not a determination of whether a report was true. Every case carries its original link so **you can check rather than trust**.

### Our interests

⑤ applied to us:

| Item | Disclosure |
|---|---|
| Revenue | This book's judgement record comes from **ad-supported blogs** (Korean and English). Clicks are needed, and criticism draws clicks. |
| Method | The pipeline is **AI-assisted**: agents draft, a human reviews and publishes. |
| Profile | The subject earns the author visibility. |
| Products | None. No paid tool or service is recommended. |
| Advertising | No sponsorship or affiliate relationship with any outlet judged here. |

This creates three biases: we **select article-worth-failing**, so accurate coverage is almost absent; it is easier to criticise outlets **without English-language originals** (verification is cheaper); and **our own defects surface late** — we, not readers, found the three failures above. Apply the eight principles to our cards too.
