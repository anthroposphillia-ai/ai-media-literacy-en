## ⑤ Disclose interests

**Definition** — Say **who is speaking and what they gain**, and separate "claimed" from "confirmed". Unsourced statistics and anonymous sources are not sources; they are **source grades**.

**Measured frequency** — **22 cards (21%)**, second most common.

### Why

AI stories carry someone's interest. Vendors publish their own benchmarks; competitors warn about each other's risks; adopting companies emphasise gains; companies announcing layoffs say automation was not the cause. Remove that angle and everyone looks like a disinterested observer.

| Layer | Wording |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | "X claimed / X said" |
| **Confirmation** | "confirmed by the court filing" |
| **Unconfirmed** | "according to a source" |

When all three are written in the same tone, readers upgrade the third into the first.

### Three check questions

1. Does the sentence say **A said it** or **A did it**?
2. What does the speaker **gain** here?
3. For unsourced statistics: is the **polling organisation, sample and date** given?

### Real case

> 「Kroger to cut 3,000 corporate roles」 (AP via Grocery Dive, 2026-10-12) — [original](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/albertsons-ceo-susan-morris-one-year-grocery-supermarkets/818900/)

The story circulated as *"AI cut 3,000 jobs"*. **The original undercuts its own headline**: the CEO memo mentioned technology change as background but **never named AI**, and the company made no official statement linking the cuts to AI adoption. The cuts target **corporate roles** (about 8,200 people — roughly 37%), i.e. 0.7% of 409,000 employees.

**Corrected**

> On 2026-10-12 Kroger said it will cut about 3,000 corporate roles over three years. The memo cited technology change as context but **did not name AI**, and the company has not linked the cuts to AI adoption. The target is corporate staff — about 37% of 8,200 — not stores or distribution centres.

### Correction formula

> `On [date], [role/name] at [organisation] claimed [content] in [document/venue]. This statement is attached to [interest], and [confirmation or rebuttal] is still absent.`

### So what

- **If you read news** — mark "according to a source" as *unconfirmed*; check whether a denial follows later in the piece.
- **If you teach** — give two articles on one event (company statement vs executive remark) and have students tabulate *who said it*.
- **If you write** — put speaker, interest and confirmation status in the same sentence. Do not quote unsourced survey numbers at all.
