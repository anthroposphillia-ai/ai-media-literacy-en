## ⑥ Give cost and benefit together

**Definition** — Report **what was gained and what was paid**. Benefit-only coverage is a press release; cost-only coverage is a bleak forecast.

**Measured frequency** — **12 cards (11%)**.

### Why

AI coverage tends to render **costs as people and benefits as multipliers**. Layoffs, surveillance, review burden and error response appear as specific humans; productivity, speed and savings appear as abstract factors. Left alone, readers feel the cost and forget the benefit.

| Item | Benefit question | Cost question |
|---|---|---|
| Money | what was saved | what was spent to build, run, retrain |
| People | who was relieved | who carries review and supervision |
| Risk | which risk fell | which **new risk** appeared |
| Time | how much faster | how much more verification takes |

### Three check questions

1. Is there a **cost-bearing party** (who paid the money and the labour)?
2. Is a **newly created risk** mentioned?
3. Is the benefit given only as a multiple, with no conditions?

### Real case

> The Kroger story, read this time for ⑥.

**What is wrong** — the coverage carries only one side. The scale of the cuts (3,000 roles, 37% of corporate staff) is concrete, while the benefit the company expects — what is automated, what it saves, what retraining it funds — is not given as a number. The reader is left with "technology made people disappear" and no idea what the change is for.

**Corrected**

> Kroger will cut about 3,000 corporate roles (about 37% of 8,200) over three years, citing organisational reorganisation amid technology change. **The savings target and any redeployment or retraining figures were not disclosed**, nor was who will carry the review and supervision load for the systems adopted.

### Correction formula

> `Benefit: [what is gained] — [condition/source]`
> `Cost: [what is paid] — [scale/source]`
> `New risk: [what appeared] — [who handles it]`
