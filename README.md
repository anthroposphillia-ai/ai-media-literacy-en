# AI Media Literacy — The 8 Principles for Judging AI News

AI ethics principles are everywhere and the newsroom is unchanged. This book does the opposite of
declaring another principle: it takes **the record of judging 107 real AI news articles** and distils the
eight tests that kept catching them.

## What it answers

- Which parts of an "AI did X" headline can you trust?
- What is missing from a story built on a benchmark number?
- How does "according to sources" differ from "confirmed"?
- Which of two articles on the same event is the accurate one?
- How do you write this kind of judgement yourself?

## The eight principles (share of violations in the audit)

| Principle | Share |
|---|---|
| ③ Disclose technical limits | 27% |
| ⑤ Disclose interests | 21% |
| ⑦ Cite verifiable sources | 18% |
| ④ Give context | 17% |
| ⑧ Offer alternatives | 13% |
| ⑥ Give cost and benefit together | 11% |
| ① Do not anthropomorphise | 7% |
| ② Name who is responsible | 7% |

## Basis and limits

- Data: 22 audit editions, 2026-10-09 → 2026-10-17; 107 tagged cards; 18 published editions.
- Evidence is quoted as **short excerpts with links to the original**; no full articles are reproduced.
- Cases age, and three of our own calls were wrong — part 03 names them.
